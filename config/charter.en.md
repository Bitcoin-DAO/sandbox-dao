# Sandbox DAO charter

November 2026 · English (binding)

This charter says what the Sandbox DAO is for and how its members work together. Every member accepts it on joining. The technical rules live in the DAO's settings; this charter sets out the spirit and the limits. Values in square brackets are filled in at founding.

## Article 1 · Name and purpose

1. The group is called the **Sandbox DAO**.
2. It is a showcase. Its purpose is to let members of the Club Orange community learn how a Bitcoin DAO works by running one: founding it, joining it, voting, paying out from a shared treasury, changing its rules and closing it.
3. It runs on Bitcoin's test network (testnet4). Test coins have no value.

## Article 2 · What it is and what it is not

1. The Sandbox DAO is a way for people to organise. It is not a company or a registered association.
2. There are no shares, tokens, dividends or returns. Joining costs nothing and earns nothing: it gives a vote.
3. No single person can spend the treasury. The website and the software cannot spend it.

## Article 3 · Members

1. **Who can join.** Anyone aged 18 or over in the Club Orange community who wants to take part in the practice.
2. **Remote by default.** Every member takes part remotely. Founding and joining are done without meeting or calling (Article 4).
3. **Name.** A pseudonym is expected. The chosen name and every vote stay public forever.
4. **Rights.** Every member can make proposals and has one vote on every proposal. All votes count equally.
5. **Duties.** Every member:
   - votes on the practice proposals and stays until the DAO is closed;
   - gives nobody their passkey or their identity card;
   - tells the council in the BTC DAO group if they lose their card or fear someone else is using their identity.
6. **Leaving.** A member can leave at any time, with no vote and no explanation. Closed votes and their pseudonym stay in the public record.
7. **Removal.** A member can be removed by a two-thirds vote. The proposal gives the reason; the member may respond before the vote closes.

## Article 4 · Founding and joining, remotely and without a call

1. **The group.** The BTC DAO group in the Club Orange app is where members identify themselves. A person's account in that group stands for them.
2. **Council seats.** The group's organiser holds one council seat and prepares the founding. The other four seats go to the first four people who ask for one in the group. Anyone asking after them goes on a reserve list, in order.
3. **Founding.** Each founder signs up on the DAO's website and posts their short code in the group. A council member checks that the short code on the setup page matches the one the founder posted. The founding settings are published, and the DAO exists once three council members have approved them on their own phones.
4. **Deadline.** Founding is completed within [48 hours] of the first founder's sign-up. A founder who misses it signs up again.
5. **Confirm.** Once the DAO exists, every founder checks that they appear in it and posts **Confirm** in the group. Anyone who sees something wrong posts **Problem** instead.
6. **New members.** After founding, a candidate signs up, posts their short code in the group and is proposed by a member. A council member checks the short code against the one on screen before the vote is confirmed.

## Article 5 · The council

1. **Make-up.** The council has five members. Each holds one of the treasury's five keys; three are needed for every payment.
2. **Keys.** In the practice, each council member's treasury key is a test key created on their own phone when they sign up. Test keys must never hold real bitcoin. Hardware signing devices are not needed.
3. **Role.** The council carries out the members' decisions. It does not decide in their place.
4. **Duties.** Every council member:
   - signs only payments the members have approved, after checking them in full;
   - takes part in the weekly signing rounds from wherever they are;
   - checks new members' short codes and confirms changes the members have approved;
   - tells the others at once if they lose a key.
5. **Refusal.** A council member may refuse to sign an approved payment, but must give the reason publicly.
6. **Limits.** The council cannot spend without a members' vote. Any payment is visible to everyone, and the members can replace the council by a two-thirds vote.
7. **Absence.** If a council member misses more than one signing round without notice, the council proposes a council change. The treasury must never be left with only three usable keys.

## Article 6 · The treasury

1. The treasury holds only test bitcoin, in a 3-of-5 multisignature wallet on testnet4. Balance, income and payments are public.
2. It is funded from the BTC DAO Testnet Treasury and is capped at [100,000 sats].
3. **The funds may be used** only for the practice exercises in this charter.

## Article 7 · Proposals and voting

1. Any member can make a proposal. The binding text is the proposal's summary, at most 500 characters.
2. Votes are yes, no or abstain, for a full [3] days. Every vote is signed and public, and can be changed until the close.
3. A proposal is valid if at least [one third] of those entitled to vote take part; abstentions count towards quorum.
4. **Simple majority** for payments, statements and new members. **Two thirds** for removals, rule changes, council changes, changes to this charter and closing the DAO.
5. At the close a council member signs the final count, which fixes the result.
6. Discussion happens in the BTC DAO group. The DAO records the decisions.

## Article 8 · Payments

1. Approved payments go out together once a week, on [Monday], UTC.
2. Whoever receives a payment gives a testnet4 address of their own.
3. An approved payment not made within two rounds lapses, and can be proposed again.
4. The network fee may not exceed [20 sat/vB].

## Article 9 · Public record

1. Everything the Sandbox DAO decides is public: members, rules, proposals, votes, balance and payments. Anyone can check it without signing in.
2. The record is permanent. Proposals, votes and pseudonyms are signed and kept on several servers, so they cannot be changed or deleted.
3. The DAO asks for the minimum: a pseudonym and a key. It never asks for a real name, email, phone number or address.
4. On joining, every member declares that they understand and accept that their pseudonym and votes stay public forever.

## Article 10 · Conduct

1. Members treat each other with respect, even when they disagree.
2. No harassment, threats or discrimination, in the DAO or in the BTC DAO group.
3. Decisions taken by vote are respected, including by those who voted against. Anyone who disagrees can make a new proposal.

## Article 11 · Changes

1. This charter is changed by a rule-change proposal, approved by two thirds and confirmed by three council members.
2. The version in force is recorded in the DAO's settings with its digital fingerprint, so anyone can check which text applies.

## Article 12 · Closing

1. The Sandbox DAO ends with a closing proposal approved by two thirds. Closing is the last exercise of the practice.
2. The whole balance returns to the BTC DAO Testnet Treasury.
3. After closing, the DAO accepts no more proposals. The record stays available, and the repository is archived.
